# One-call close (lead -> signed -> building, same day)

Playbook doc: https://claude.ai/artifact/EgMadkknvVzK1Vbdhd864j
Copy in this repo: `rules/sop/one-call-close-sop.md` (v1, 9 Nov 2026; copied 16 Nov from the old project files). Read it in
full before a call.
Contracts are in the name of Bennett & Co LLC ("Plumber King Agency, owned by Bennett & Co LLC").

## Must-follow rules
1. **No sales call without a finished design and a pre-filled sheet.** No call ends without (a) signed + paid + the 15
   must-haves collected, or (b) a clear no. "I'll send you some documents" is not an outcome.
2. **Start the pre-call pack the moment Nick says a lead is interested or a call is booked, without being asked**
   (Nick's own standing rule, 9 Nov: "remember at all times so that when the next client comes, we are always ready").
3. **Never set prices, packages, the guarantee line or timeline numbers for Nick.** Those are his decisions (open since
   9 Nov, see project memory `open-items.md`). Nothing priced can be sent until he sets them.
4. Never promise a number of calls. Say the guarantee once, the same way every time.
5. Plumbers only; flag a non-plumber lead before doing any work.

## Why (what went wrong with Jim, 6 Nov call, facts)
46-minute call, ~35 min persuading (Google vs social), no logistics collected, free work + no contract + no money + no dated
next step, mixed message on guarantees, then 3 rounds of forms (44, 49, 27 questions) and nothing came back. Another agency
closed him in one call (9 Nov). A lead who has signed and paid nothing can walk away for free.

## Before the call (2-4 hours, Claude does it)
1. **Research:** website, Google profile (rating + count), reviews, licence on the state board, insurance mentions, domain
   owner, socials, logo, services and towns already listed.
2. **Figma homepage**, desktop + mobile, in the client's look, per the `pka-client-site` skill and the homepage CRO SOP.
   Honest limit: 2-4 h buys a strong homepage, not a full site; the full site comes after signing.
3. **Pre-fill the 15-item must-have sheet** (below) from research, so on the call he only confirms.
4. **Ready to send while on the call:** proposal one-pager, contract (Bennett & Co LLC), payment link, after-call form link,
   shared Drive folder. Blocked until Nick sets price, packages, payment tool and e-sign tool.
5. **Red-flag check**, one line each: no licence, no Google profile, bad reviews, not a plumber. Nick decides if the call
   still happens.

## The call (30 minutes)
- **0-2 min, frame:** "I'll ask a few questions, show you the website I already designed for you, tell you exactly what you
  get and what it costs, and if it's a fit we get you started today. Sound good?"
- **2-7 min, 3 questions only:** how do most jobs come in now; how many jobs a month do you want / can handle; what have you
  tried that didn't work. Don't debate channels: "Fair. I do one thing: Google and your website. Let me show you."
- **7-14 min, show the design:** walk the homepage in 5 minutes; point to his real name, photos, towns; "What would you
  change?" Write it down.
- **14-19 min, the offer:** what he gets (scope = Nick's decision per client), when (Nick's numbers), what he owns (domain,
  hosting, Google profile in his name), price (say once, stop talking), guarantee line (once).
- **19-27 min, close + must-haves:** "I'm sending the agreement and the payment link now, so we can start building today."
  Send both on the call; run the sheet while he signs.
- **27-30 min, lock the next step:** files by [day], first page on [day]; put the date in WhatsApp before hanging up.
- **Pushback:** "Let me think" -> "What's the one thing you're unsure about?" · "Just send it" -> "I will right now, while
  we're on." · "Guarantee calls?" -> the guarantee line, unchanged. · "No contract" -> "It protects you too: it says you
  own everything." Free work: at least a signed one-page agreement + a small paid commitment (e.g. he pays domain + hosting).

## The 15-item must-have sheet (ask ON the call, pre-filled)
Business
1. Business name as customers see it, and how it's registered (LLC, Inc, DBA).
2. The one phone number for site + Google, business email, WhatsApp.
3. Base address: home or shop? Show or hide on Google?
4. Hours: truly 24/7? Usual emergency arrival time?
Legal and trust
5. Licence number (or exam date). If none, Claude checks that state's rules before any page goes live.
6. Insured? What type?
7. Where his reviews live (Google, Yelp, Facebook, video). Any he can't use?
Jobs and areas
8. Confirm the service list. Any he doesn't do?
9. Top 3 jobs he wants more of.
10. Top 10 towns he wants calls from, and any he doesn't.
Accounts (live if possible)
11. Domain: owns one? Where bought? Who has the login? If none, pick the name now.
12. Google Business Profile: exists? Name/phone/email used? Who has the login?
13. Old website: who controls it? Take it down at launch?
Brand and approval
14. Logo and photos: has them, where? (Files come after the call.)
15. Who approves the design, and the fastest way to reach him.

## After the call (same day)
1. Nick sends the Loom link or notes; Claude pulls every answer into the client file (project memory `clients.md` + client folder)
   and the Figma thread.
2. Send the after-call form with only what's left: story, crew names, warranty, payment methods, financing, call-out fee,
   price ranges on site, Spanish, awards, colours, sites he likes, job goals.
3. Send the file list with the Drive folder: logo, 3+ photos per service, 20 best job photos, crew and trucks, review
   screenshots, registration paper.
4. Next: full Figma per `pka-client-site`. Open question for Nick: the SOP says "build starts the same day", while the site
   rule says never build before Nick approves the full Figma. Until he settles it, the Figma approval gate wins.
   Missing files get clearly labelled stand-ins, never invented facts.
