## The 12 rules every script must pass
1. **Hook - Retain - Reward** (FACT, $100M Leads). Hook = first 1-3 s. Retain = curiosity, list or steps. Reward = the promise paid off. Big hook + tiny payoff = fail.
2. **Callout first** (FACT, paid-ads chapter): name who it's for in the first line. "Plumbers," "If you run a plumbing company," "Plumber in Phoenix...". Hormozi: "Always call out who you're looking for."
3. **Hook = 80% of the work** (FACT: "After you've written your headline, you've spent 80 cents of your advertising dollar"). Write 5 hooks per script, keep the best. Hook rules he uses: "curiosity is king, different is ideal."
4. **Proof beats promise** (FACT: "Proof will always outdo promise"). Show the real site, real number, real review count on screen. Never claim without showing.
5. **Value per second / no such thing as too long, only too boring** (FACT). Cut every line that doesn't teach or move the story. One idea per reel.
6. **"How I" beats "How to"** (FACT, 7 content lessons). "Here's how I fixed..." / "I'm replacing this button..." beats "You should...". Ed's best non-offer reel (4,501 views) is exactly this: "I'm replacing this call icon..."
7. **Narrow, for the buyer, not for creators** (FACT: "for us -> for you", "wide -> narrow"). Every reel is about a plumber's calls, jobs, money, Google. No lifestyle, no agency inside talk.
8. **Assume nothing / talk to a stranger** (FACT). Every reel works for someone who never saw Nick. A 2-second "who I am" is fine ("I build sites for plumbers").
9. **Plain words, short sentences, 3rd-grade level** (FACT: "Fancy equals friction, simple equals sales"). No "CTA", "conversion", "UX", "SEO" unless explained in the same breath.
10. **Describe the moment, not the benefit** (FACT: "the moments is what they remember"). "2am, water on the kitchen floor, she taps your number and nothing happens."
11. **Always give a reason why ("because")** (FACT).
12. **Give far more than you ask; one ask at a time** (FACT: "give until they ask"; "a confused mind doesn't buy"). Most reels: no ask. When there is one: ONE comment word for a free thing (lead magnet). Hormozi: "If you're not sure, do the lead magnet."

## Volume and testing rules
- **Volume first** (FACT: Rule of 100; "more" before "better" before "new"). Judge a format after many posts, not 3.
- **Double down on outliers** (FACT): "If a post does better than normal, make more content on that topic." Re-post / remake winners; new viewers haven't seen them.
- **Make content like what already wins on the platform, then aim it at your buyer** (FACT).
- **Proof content**: turn client results into stories, testimonials, case studies (FACT). United Plumbing (first client) = the biggest content asset Nick has. Film the process.

INFER: the formats Hormozi pushes (free offer, proof, "how I") are exactly Ed's top formats. Plain audits and talking-head tips are his weakest. So: every audit should be framed as a "how I'd fix it" or before/after, not just "this site is bad".

## Script checklist (run on every script before delivery)
- [ ] Line 1 calls out plumbers or a plumber situation
- [ ] Hook names a specific, visible thing (number, button, review count), not a vague claim
- [ ] Shows proof on screen within 3 s
- [ ] One idea only
- [ ] Contains a "because" reason
- [ ] Describes a real customer moment
- [ ] Payoff is a takeaway the WATCHING plumber can use on his own site today
- [ ] No jargon; read aloud in under 25 s (audits) / 35 s (edu)
- [ ] Max one ask; if any, a free thing via one comment word
- [ ] 5 hook options written, best one picked
