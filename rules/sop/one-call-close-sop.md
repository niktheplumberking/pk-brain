# One-Call Close: lead to signed to building, same day

Plumber King Agency (owned by Bennett & Co LLC). v1, 9 Nov 2026. Born from losing United Plumbing (Jim) on 9 Nov.

**The rule:** no sales call without a finished design and a pre-filled sheet. No call ends without one of two outcomes: (a) signed + paid + the 15 must-haves collected, or (b) a clear no. "I'll send you some documents" is not an outcome.

---

## 1. What went wrong with Jim (from the 6 Nov call recording)

- **Fact:** the recording is 46 minutes. Roughly the first 35 were spent arguing Google vs social media and persuading him. Logistics only started near the end.
- **Fact:** no domain, logo, photos, Google login, phone number or service-area list was collected on the call.
- **Fact:** Nick offered a contract; Jim said no because the work was free ("if there's no money involved, I don't want a contract yet"). The call ended with "I'll contact you on WhatsApp as soon as I have everything ready."
- **Fact:** after the call it took 3 rounds of forms (44 questions, then 49, then a 27-question call sheet) and nothing came back before he left.
- **Fact:** Nick said "I'm not guaranteeing you that" and then "I can guarantee you more" about 1 call a week. Mixed message.
- **I'm inferring:** the main loss was not missing questions. It was that the deal locked nothing in: free, no contract, no payment, no next step with a date. The other agency asked for the yes, the signature and (very likely) money on the same call. A client who has paid nothing can walk away for free.

## 2. Before the call (2 to 4 hours, Claude does it)

1. **Research** (Claude): website, Google profile (rating + count), reviews, licence lookup on the state board, insurance mentions, domain owner (who bought it, where), social links, logo pulled from site/IG, services and towns already listed.
2. **Figma homepage**, desktop + mobile, in the client's look, following the Figma SOP. **Honest limit:** 2 to 4 hours buys a strong homepage, not a full site. The United full site took most of a day on top of the homepage. Full site comes after signing.
3. **Pre-fill the must-have sheet** (section 4) with everything research found, so on the call he only says "yes, right" or corrects it.
4. **Ready to send while still on the call:** proposal one-pager, contract (Bennett & Co LLC), payment link, after-call form link, shared Drive folder for files. **Nick's decision:** price, packages, payment tool, e-sign tool. Nothing here can be sent until those are set.
5. **Red-flag check** (Claude, 1 line each): no licence, no Google profile, bad reviews, not a plumber. Nick decides if the call still happens.

## 3. The call script (30 minutes)

**0-2 min · Set the frame.**
"Thanks for jumping on. Here's the plan for the next 30 minutes: I'll ask you a few questions, show you the website I already designed for you, tell you exactly what you get and what it costs, and if it's a fit we get you started today. Sound good?"

**2-7 min · His situation (3 questions only).**
1. "How do most of your jobs come in right now?"
2. "How many jobs a month do you want, and how many can you handle today?"
3. "What have you tried before that didn't work?"
Do not debate marketing channels. If he pushes ("social is the future"), say: "Fair. I do one thing: Google and your website. Let me show you."

**7-14 min · Show the design.** Share screen. Walk the homepage top to bottom in 5 minutes. Point to his real name, real photos, real towns. Ask: "What would you change?" Write it down.

**14-19 min · The offer.**
- What he gets: the site, Google Business Profile setup, ongoing care. (Scope list = Nick's decision per client.)
- When: "Design approved in X days, live in Y days" (Nick's numbers).
- What he owns: domain, hosting, Google profile, all in his name.
- Price: **Nick's decision.** Say it once, plainly, then stop talking.
- Guarantee: say once, the same way every time: "I can't promise a number of calls, nobody honestly can. What I promise is X." (Nick sets X.)

**19-27 min · Close, then the must-haves.**
"Does this sound like what you want? Great. I'm sending the agreement and the payment link now, so we can start building today." Send both while on the call. While he signs, run the must-have sheet (section 4).

**27-30 min · Lock the next step.**
"You'll get a short form for the rest, and a folder for photos. Can you drop your logo and photos in by [day]? I'll send the first page on [day]." Put the date in WhatsApp before hanging up.

**Short answers to the usual pushback**
- "Let me think about it." → "Sure. What's the one thing you're unsure about?" Answer that one thing, then ask again.
- "Just send it over." → "I will right now, while we're on, so you can ask me anything as you read it."
- "Can you guarantee calls?" → Use the guarantee line above. Never change it on the call.
- "I don't want a contract." → "It protects you too: it says you own everything." If no money is changing hands, see section 6.

## 4. Must-have sheet: ask these ON the call (15 items)

Everything Claude already found is pre-filled; he only confirms.

**Business**
1. Business name exactly as customers should see it, and how it's registered (LLC, Inc, DBA).
2. The one phone number for the site and Google, plus business email and WhatsApp.
3. Base address: home or shop? Show it or hide it on Google?
4. Hours: truly 24/7? Usual emergency arrival time?

**Legal and trust**
5. Licence number (or exam date if none). If none, Claude checks that state's rules before any page goes live.
6. Insured? What type?
7. Where his reviews live today (Google, Yelp, Facebook, video). Any he can't use?

**Jobs and areas**
8. Confirm the service list (pre-filled). Any he doesn't do?
9. Top 3 jobs he wants more of.
10. Top 10 towns he wants calls from, and any he doesn't.

**Accounts (do it live if you can)**
11. Domain: does he own one? Where was it bought? Who has the login? If none: pick the name now.
12. Google Business Profile: does one exist? Name, phone and email used? Who has the login?
13. Old website: who controls it? Take it down at launch?

**Brand and approval**
14. Logo and photos: does he have them, and where? (Files come after the call.)
15. Who approves the design, and the fastest way to reach him.

## 5. After the call (same day)

1. Nick sends the Loom link or call notes. Claude pulls every answer into the client file and the Figma thread.
2. Send the **after-call form** with only what's left (from the 44-question list): story, crew names, warranty, payment methods, financing, call-out fee, price ranges on site, Spanish, awards, colours, sites he likes, job goals.
3. Send the **file list** with the Drive folder: logo, 3+ photos per service, 20 best job photos, crew and trucks, review screenshots, registration paper.
4. Build starts the same day from the call answers. Missing files get stand-ins clearly labelled, never invented facts.

## 6. Decisions only Nick can make (needed before the next call)

1. Price and packages (what's in each).
2. Free vs paid. **Hard truth:** free with no contract lost Jim. If you keep doing free work for testimonials, at least get a signed one-page agreement (what you do, what he gives: video review, logins, files by a date) and a small paid commitment (for example he pays domain + hosting on the call).
3. Payment tool (for example a Stripe or PayPal link) and e-sign tool. **I don't know** which you already have set up.
4. The guarantee line: one sentence, same on every call.
5. Timeline numbers: design approved in X days, live in Y days.
